# Fly Brain Codex demo reel

[Watch or download the finished narrated reel](https://github.com/monomyth/fly-brain-codex/releases/download/v0.1.0/fly-brain-codex.mp4) · [English captions](https://github.com/monomyth/fly-brain-codex/releases/download/v0.1.0/fly-brain-codex.srt)

A 74.8-second, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps reel assembled and rendered in DaVinci Resolve Studio 21.1 through Blackmagic’s native MCP. Seven visual dissolves, nine generated narration lines edited into twelve timed clips, 25 styled burned-in subtitles, and native music gain changes/crossfades form the edit. The transition recipe was adapted from the installed MagicScript catalogue.

The real simulator demonstration is an Orbit spectator recording at normal speed. The actor still uses its fixed Front and Gripper RGB observations. It starts folded with a 20 mm cube near X350/Y0 mm, uses the retained baseline for pickup and hold, then executes a scripted release and landing check. [Selected trial measurements](evidence/demo-run.json).

This is a selected successful filming take. Two subsequent takes missed the grasp. These convenience recordings are not a planned qualification and do not update the historical 19/20 baseline or 12/15 experimental-model counts. [Filming records](evidence/orbit-filming.json).

The reel identifies the newer experimental controller’s limitations and labels token/time figures as rough log-based estimates including cached context and tool waits. It does not claim biological fidelity or broad robot reliability.

Narration: ElevenLabs stock **Liam**, generated from the explanatory script. Music: **Tiny Gears, Big Dreams**, generated with Suno and supplied by the project author. MaleCNS geometry: derived soma positions, credited to FlyEM/Janelia, Cambridge, MRC-LMB, Google and collaborators under CC BY 4.0; the rotating geometry shot does not depict measured neuronal activity.

The subtitles use editable native Fusion Text+ compositions: bold condensed type, colored action labels, rounded accent frames and short fades. The SRT/VTT timing matches the rendered captions. Camera images use equal dimensions and margins; hold and release narration were aligned to inspected action frames.
